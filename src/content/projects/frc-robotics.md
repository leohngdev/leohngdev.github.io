## Where I learned to debug

This is the oldest thing on my CV and the reason I am any good at debugging.

Software on a robot cannot lie to you comfortably. Once it is on the field there is no console to
read, only a machine doing the wrong thing while a match clock runs. Sensors return noisy values, the
same code behaves differently on carpet than on tile, and a fault can be mechanical, electrical or
yours. You learn to form one specific hypothesis and test it instead of changing something plausible
and hoping.

I used that habit years later at ANTSA to find a bug that produced no error and no log entry, only
wrong data. Same discipline: stop trusting the layer that looks fine, follow the values.

## The other half

I also explained our software decisions to mentors. Presenting to someone who can stop you with a
hard question is a good forcing function, because you cannot hand-wave through an explanation of
something you do not understand.
