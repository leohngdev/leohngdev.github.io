## The engineering in art

Making something look right in a still render is one problem. Making it hold up while it moves, on a
budget, is a different one — and it is much closer to software work than people tend to assume.

Topology is the clearest example. Where you place edges decides whether a joint creases correctly or
collapses when it bends. You are designing for a use case you cannot fully see at build time,
spending a limited resource deliberately, and living with those decisions later when changing them
is expensive. That is a familiar shape of problem.

Low-poly work makes the tradeoff explicit. Every polygon is a choice about where detail earns its
cost, and the discipline of asking that question repeatedly is the same instinct that makes me care
about payload size and query count on the web.

## Where it connects

This is the reason I am comfortable working with designers and artists rather than treating them as
a source of tickets. Having been on the other side of a handoff, I know what makes an asset pipeline
painful, and I have a realistic sense of what a "small change" actually costs.
